## T001 interviewer (scope) [SCOPE]
You're the expert here. I don't know your shop, so I'll ask a lot of how and why questions. Nothing becomes shop knowledge until you approve it. Should we focus on outside processing, or something else?

## T002 expert
Outside processing is good. It's where a quote looks fine on paper and then the job loses money after it leaves the building.

## T003 interviewer (task_map) [TASK_MAP]
If you broke quoting a job with outside processing into three to six big steps, what would they be?

## T004 expert
Read the print and the notes for every process callout. Figure out the sequence, what gets done before and after the vendor. Get the vendor quotes. Put in the travel time. Then price our own hours around all of it.

## T005 interviewer (task_map) [TASK_MAP]
Which of those steps takes the most experience to get right?

## T006 expert
The sequence. What you finish before heat treat and what you leave for after. If you get that wrong, the vendor quote doesn't matter.

## T007 interviewer (incident) [INCIDENT]
Think of a specific recent job where the sequence really mattered. Which job comes to mind?

## T008 expert
The Halvorsen end cap last October. Twenty-five pieces, 17-4, heat treated after machining.

## T009 interviewer (timeline) [TIMELINE]
Walk me through that job from when it landed on your desk to when it shipped.

## T010 expert
I quoted it with the bores finished before heat treat to save a setup. The parts went out to the heat treater, came back, and the bores had moved out of round. Linda caught it at incoming. We added a finish op after heat treat on every part, and the heat treater was a week late on top of that.

## T011 interviewer (timeline) [TIMELINE]
Here's what I have: you quoted the bores finished before heat treat, the parts came back with the bores out of round, Linda caught it, you added a finish op after heat treat, and the vendor was a week late. What did I miss or get out of order?

## T012 expert
That's it. The quote was the mistake, not the shop floor.

## T013 interviewer (deepen) [ASK_WHY]
Why does finishing before heat treat matter so much on a part like that?

## T014 expert
The part moves when it comes out of the furnace. Not a lot, but on a close bore it's enough. Anything tight on 17-4 that gets heat treated, you leave stock and finish it after.

## T015 interviewer (deepen) [ASK_HOW_TELL]
How can you tell from the print that a feature is one of the ones to leave for after?

## T016 expert
Look at the tolerance on the bores and faces, and look for the heat treat note. If it's a close bore and there's a heat treat callout, that bore gets finished after. The print won't tell you the order. You have to decide it.

## T017 interviewer (deepen) [ASK_EXCEPTION]
When would you not leave it for after?

## T018 expert
If the tolerance is loose, or it's a feature nobody measures closely, I don't pay for the extra op.

## T019 interviewer (deepen) [KA_PROBE]
Is there a trick you use when you get the vendor quotes?

## T020 expert
Always get the outside processing quote in writing before ours goes out — price and lead time. A number over the phone from the heat treater isn't a quote. When I skip that, the lead time is what bites.

## T021 interviewer (what_if) [WHAT_IF]
If the heat treater had quoted you in writing with the real lead time, would your call on that job have changed?

## T022 expert
The price wouldn't have changed much. The ship date would have. I'd have told Halvorsen a later date up front instead of calling them late.

## T023 interviewer (novice_gap) [NOVICE_GAP]
What would a new hire most likely get wrong on a job like the end cap?

## T024 expert
They'd take the vendor price and forget the sequence. Or they'd forget the travel days both ways. They treat outside processing like a line item instead of a step in the job.

## T025 interviewer (teach_back) [TEACH_BACK]
Let me say it back as a rule: anything tight on 17-4 that gets heat treated, you leave stock and finish it after, and you get the outside processing quote in writing before ours goes out — price and lead time. What did I get wrong or leave out?

## T026 expert
Add the travel days. Otherwise that's right. The end cap is the story I tell every new quoter.
